Today's overview: With 22 days to go before the May 19 primary, the PA-01 congressional race continues to dominate Bucks County coverage as Republican incumbent Brian Fitzpatrick maintains a roughly $7M cash-on-hand advantage over the Democratic field. Lucia Simonelli is leaning further into a progressive "hopes-not-fears" message in a fresh Bucks County Beacon profile, while frontrunner Bob Harvie is touring the district with town halls. Down-ballot, the race for the 16th State Senate District has solidified into a contested Democratic primary among Mark Pinsley, Bradley Merkl-Gump, and others — a contest progressives say is critical to flipping the chamber. Eileen Hartnett Albillar continues to consolidate Democratic establishment support in the 6th, and HD-142 challenger Kristin Egan has become the first state-level candidate to publicly oppose PennDOT's $227M US-1 reconstruction.

U.S. Congress — 1st Congressional District

Bob Harvie · Falls Township

Bucks County Commissioner who announced his congressional bid in April 2025. A lifelong Bucks County resident born in Bristol Borough, he graduated magna cum laude from George Washington University and spent 20 years as a teacher and Social Studies Department chair at Bucks County Technical High School. Elected County Commissioner in 2019, named board chair in 2022, and re-elected in 2023, his campaign centers on economic fairness and kitchen-table issues.

Brian Fitzpatrick reported $7.66M cash on hand at the end of Q1 — more than any other PA congressional candidate — while Harvie raised nearly $416,000 in Q1 and finished March with about $603,000 in the bank, the largest war chest among Democratic challengers in the field.

Side-by-side voter-guide profiles of the two leading PA-01 Democrats. Harvie emphasizes pocketbook issues — raising the federal minimum wage, supporting union workers, repealing Trump's tariffs, and codifying middle-class tax relief — and is positioned as the frontrunner with broad institutional backing.

Harvie's continuing town-hall tour stops in Middletown Township, focusing on affordability, healthcare costs, and federal cuts. The event is the fifth in a series the campaign is using to introduce Harvie to general-election swing voters in the lower county.

Lucia Simonelli · Quakertown Borough

Holds a PhD in mathematics and has served as a scientific advisor in the U.S. Senate. Born in Lansdale and now settled in Quakertown, she directs programs for a nonprofit focused on scaling clean-air technologies. Running as a progressive, she is zeroing in on universal healthcare and Medicare for All.

A campaign profile making the case for Simonelli's progressive lane: Medicare for All, climate action, abolishing ICE, and a "hopes-not-fears" framing that positions her as the bold-vision alternative to Harvie's centrist pitch in the closing stretch of the primary.

Steve Santarsiero · 10th Senatorial District (Incumbent)

Has represented the 10th District since 2019 and serves as chair of the Bucks County Democrats. Running unopposed in the May primary, he remains active on the minimum wage, PECO rate increases, and other legislative issues.

Pennsylvania Senate Democrats — Statement on PECO's Proposed Rate Increase (Apr 24, 2026)
Santarsiero issued a statement opposing PECO's proposed rate hike, arguing the increase would push working families further behind at a time when affordability is the top issue facing Bucks households.

Eileen Hartnett Albillar · 6th Senatorial District

Bucks County Clerk of Courts who launched her bid in January 2026 to unseat Republican Frank Farry. A former Warrington Township supervisor with a background in social work and nonprofit leadership, she has been endorsed by Senate Democratic Leader Jay Costa, Sen. Santarsiero, and Commissioners Harvie and Ellis-Marseglia. Her campaign focuses on affordability, SEPTA funding, and working families.

A Democratic primary contest for a seat that strategists describe as essential to flipping the state Senate. Mark Pinsley (Lehigh County Controller) and Bradley Merkl-Gump (Pennridge School Board director) headline the field, joined by Wayne Codner of Richlandtown Borough.
