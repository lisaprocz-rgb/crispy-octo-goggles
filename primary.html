Executive Summary

With 21 days until the May 19 primary, Bucks County's two PA-01 Democratic congressional candidates — Bob Harvie and Lucia Simonelli — squared off in their highest-profile face-off yet at a League of Women Voters forum in Middletown Township on April 27. Coverage in The Inquirer, WHYY, and the Bucks County Beacon dissected the contrast between Harvie's pragmatic moderate pitch and Simonelli's progressive Medicare-for-All platform. Down-ballot, HD-142 challenger Kristin Egan continued to draw attention for her opposition to the $227M US-1 reconstruction project, while the PECO rate-hike fight that united Bucks County Democratic legislators ended with PECO's CEO stepping aside and the proposal withdrawn.

U.S. Congress — 1st Congressional District

Bob Harvie — Falls Township

Bucks County Commissioner who announced his congressional bid in April 2025. Lifelong Bucks County resident born in Bristol Borough, graduated magna cum laude from George Washington University, spent 20 years as a teacher and Social Studies Department chair at Bucks County Technical High School. Elected County Commissioner in 2019, named board chair in 2022, re-elected in 2023. Campaign centers on economic fairness and kitchen-table issues.

The Philadelphia Inquirer — Bucks County's Bob Harvie and Lucia Simonelli face off in candidate forum ahead of Democratic primary (April 28, 2026)

Harvie, the local-party-endorsed candidate, leaned on his record as county commissioner and his fundraising lead ($1.35M to Simonelli's ~$50K) during a nearly two-hour forum at Middletown Township, drawing contrasts on health care, data centers and campaign money.

WHYY — Pa. election 2026: Meet Bob Harvie and Lucia Simonelli, Dems fighting to unseat Brian Fitzpatrick (April 27, 2026)

Voter-guide profile pegs Harvie as the moderate front-runner focused on raising the federal minimum wage, supporting union workers, repealing Trump-era tariffs, and middle-class tax relief.

Central Bucks News — Fitzpatrick has $7M more on hand than Democrats challenging him (April 23, 2026)

Q1 FEC filings show Harvie has raised $1.35M since launching but trails incumbent Brian Fitzpatrick's roughly $7M cash-on-hand by a wide margin heading into the home stretch of the primary.

LevittownNow.com — Democratic Congressional Candidates Taking Part In Forum (April 25, 2026)

Preview of the April 27 League of Women Voters forum, noting Harvie has yet to RSVP to a separate May 8 Upper Bucks United forum in Quakertown that Simonelli has confirmed.

Lucia Simonelli — Quakertown Borough

Holds a PhD in mathematics and has served as a scientific advisor in the U.S. Senate. Born in Lansdale, now settled in Quakertown. Directs programs for a nonprofit focused on scaling clean-air technologies. Running as a progressive, zeroing in on universal healthcare and Medicare for All.

Bucks County Beacon — Lucia Simonelli Wants PA-01 Democrats to Vote Their Hopes, Not Their Fears (April 26, 2026)

Long-form profile makes the case for Simonelli's progressive platform — Medicare for All, climate action, abolishing ICE — and frames her grassroots, small-dollar strategy as a bet that Democrats are ready to vote on aspiration rather than electability.

The Philadelphia Inquirer — Bucks County's Bob Harvie and Lucia Simonelli face off in candidate forum ahead of Democratic primary (April 28, 2026)

Simonelli pressed her contrast with Harvie on universal health care and on accepting corporate PAC money during the forum, repeatedly framing the primary as a choice between incrementalism and bold reform.

Bucks County Beacon — OPINION: PA-01 Democrats Finally Practice Democracy in Their Democratic Primary (April 24, 2026)

Op-ed argues that a contested Harvie–Simonelli primary is healthy for the local party and an overdue exercise in member-driven democracy after years of pre-cleared candidacies.

Pennsylvania State Senate

Steve Santarsiero — 10th Senatorial District (Incumbent)

Has represented the 10th District since 2019 and serves as chair of the Bucks County Democrats. Running unopposed in the May primary. Active on minimum wage, PECO rate increases, and other legislative issues.

New Hope Free Press — PECO CEO Moving On After Failed Rate Hike (April 22, 2026)

Santarsiero was among the loudest Democratic voices opposing PECO's 12.5% electric / 11.4% gas rate hike; the proposal was withdrawn April 16 and PECO's CEO announced his exit days later.

LevittownNow.com — After Outcry, PECO Withdraws Request For Price Hikes (April 16, 2026)

Bipartisan pressure — including from Santarsiero and Bucks County Democratic House members — was credited with PECO's reversal under pressure from Gov. Shapiro.

Eileen Hartnett Albillar — 6th Senatorial District

Bucks County Clerk of Courts who launched her bid in January 2026 to unseat Republican Frank Farry. Former Warrington Township supervisor with a background in social work and nonprofit leadership. Endorsed by Senate Democratic Leader Jay Costa, Sen. Santarsiero, Commissioners Harvie and Ellis-Marseglia. Focuses on affordability, SEPTA funding, and working families.

Bucks County Herald — 2026 General Primary candidate listing (updated) (March 27, 2026)

Per Bucks County's official candidate listing, Hartnett Albillar is unopposed on the Democratic side; she will face GOP incumbent Frank Farry, also unopposed in his primary, in November.

No new coverage in the last 48 hours.

16th Senatorial District — Mark Pinsley vs. Bradley Merkl-Gump

Contested primary between Mark Pinsley (Lehigh County Controller) and Bradley Merkl-Gump (Pennridge School Board). A key race for flipping the state Senate; the winner faces Republican Sen. Jarrett Coleman in November.

LehighValleyNews.com — Pinsley, Merkl-Gump to go head-to-head in 16th state Senate District's Democratic primary debate (April 22, 2026)

Preview of the only scheduled primary debate, framing Pinsley's eighth campaign in 10 years against Merkl-Gump's first state-level run as a generational and stylistic contrast.

Armchair Lehigh Valley — Two Democrats left in 16th Senate District's primary (April 18, 2026)

Reports that the field narrowed to Pinsley and Merkl-Gump after petition challenges and dropouts; both candidates have been campaigning across Bucks and Lehigh counties.

Pennsylvania State House

Bryan Allen — 18th District (Bensalem)

Former Bensalem Township council member and chief of staff for State Rep. Tina Davis, 47. Introduced the first casino tax rebate for Bensalem residents. Vice chair of Bucks County Water and Sewer Authority Board. Challenging Republican K.C. Tomlinson.

Local outlet's running coverage of the Allen-Tomlinson general-election matchup highlights Allen's platform of expanding healthcare access, raising the minimum wage, and prioritizing public-school funding.

First elected to the General Assembly in 2016; represents Lower Makefield, Newtown Borough, Newtown Township, Yardley and Upper Makefield. Unopposed in the primary.

Tina Davis — 141st District (Bristol Township), Incumbent

Has represented Bristol Township and Bristol Borough since 2010. Priorities include economic development, pay equity, property tax reform. Currently serves as House Majority Caucus Secretary.

Davis joined fellow Bucks County Democratic House members in formally opposing PECO's rate hikes; she also faces Republican Kenneth James Velez of Bristol Township in November.

Kristin Egan — 142nd District (Langhorne)

Neshaminy School Board director and 20-year public school teacher challenging Republican Joe Hogan. First state candidate to publicly oppose $227M PennDOT US-1 reconstruction project. Platform: education funding reform, public safety, cost of living.

Bucks County Beacon — Democratic State House Candidate Kristin Egan Goes on Record to Oppose Controversial US 1 Project in Lower Bucks County (April 17, 2026)

Egan became the first state-level candidate to formally oppose the $227M PennDOT US-1 reconstruction, citing community-impact concerns and using the issue to draw a contrast with incumbent Republican Joe Hogan.

No new coverage in the last 48 hours.

Tim Hayes — 143rd District (Dublin)

Dublin Borough Council president, Army National Guard veteran, environmental policy expert running against Republican Shelby Labs. Priorities include alternative energy, regulating AI data farms, affordability, education.

Bucks County Beacon — OPINION: State House Bill Seeks to Protect Pennsylvania Communities from Data Center Impacts (April 23, 2026)

Hayes uses an op-ed to spotlight HB 501 (Alternative Energy Portfolio Standards Act), notes opponent Shelby Labs voted against it, and argues AI data center operators — not residents — should bear rising energy costs.

No new coverage in the last 48 hours.

Brian Munroe — 144th District (Warminster), Incumbent

Former Radnor Township police officer and Warminster Township supervisor. Unseated Republican Todd Polinchock in 2022 by 515 votes, re-elected in 2024 by 1,065 votes. Endorsed by Conservation Voters of PA. Swing district.

PA House Democratic Caucus — Bucks County lawmakers say public mobilization was instrumental in PECO's decision to rescind its proposed rate hike (April 17, 2026)

Munroe credited grassroots organizing for forcing PECO's reversal; he had warned that the proposed hikes plus SEPTA service cuts would compound costs for Warminster Regional Rail Line commuters.

No new coverage in the last 48 hours; he faces Republican Mike Murphy of Warrington in November.

Les Mavus — 145th District (Nockamixon)

Nockamixon Township resident and inspector of elections challenging Republican Craig Staats.

25-year public school science teacher and Science Department Chair at Wissahickon School District. Auditor in Warwick Township — first Democrat elected to that office in decades. Challenging Republican Kristin Marcell.
