Executive Summary

With 23 days until the May 19 primary, the marquee race remains PA-01, where Bob Harvie and Lucia Simonelli square off in a Democratic congressional primary heading into a League of Women Voters debate Monday night in Doylestown. Reporting this week underscores Republican incumbent Brian Fitzpatrick's $7M+ cash advantage, while a major Democratic super PAC commits $20M to PA U.S. House races. Down-ballot, contested fights in the 16th Senate District and key Bucks state House swing seats continue to draw attention as Democrats push to flip both chambers in Harrisburg.

U.S. Congress — 1st Congressional District

Bob Harvie — Falls Township

Bucks County Commissioner who announced his congressional bid in April 2025. A lifelong Bucks County resident born in Bristol Borough, Harvie graduated magna cum laude from George Washington University and spent 20 years as a teacher and Social Studies Department chair at Bucks County Technical High School. Elected County Commissioner in 2019, he was named board chair in 2022 and re-elected in 2023. His campaign centers on economic fairness and kitchen-table issues.

LevittownNow.com — "Democratic Congressional Candidates Taking Part In Forum" (Apr 25, 2026)

The League of Women Voters of Bucks County will host Harvie and Simonelli at a Democratic primary forum Monday, April 27, from 7–9 p.m. at the Middletown Township Municipal Complex in Langhorne — the only scheduled head-to-head appearance before the May 19 vote.

Central Bucks News — "Fitzpatrick has $7M more on hand than Democrats challenging him" (Apr 23, 2026)

New Q1 FEC filings show Harvie raised just under $430,000 from January through March and has banked roughly $1.35M total, but Republican incumbent Brian Fitzpatrick still entered April with $7.65M cash on hand — a $7M+ gap heading into the general.

The Philadelphia Inquirer — "Major Democratic super PAC commits $20M for PA U.S. House races" (Apr 23, 2026)

House Majority PAC announced a $20M Pennsylvania commitment focused on competitive U.S. House districts including PA-01, signaling national Democratic prioritization of the Harvie-Fitzpatrick general-election race.

PoliticsPA — "Can Bob Harvie End Brian Fitzpatrick's Tenure in Congress?" (Apr 2026)

An analytic profile of Harvie as the Democratic establishment's choice — backed by former congressmen Patrick Murphy and Peter Kostmayer and prior nominees Ashley Ehasz and Scott Wallace — and the structural challenges he faces in the perennially purple PA-01.

Lucia Simonelli — Quakertown Borough

Holds a PhD in mathematics and has served as a scientific advisor in the U.S. Senate. Born in Lansdale and now settled in Quakertown, Simonelli directs programs for a nonprofit focused on scaling clean-air technologies. She is running as a progressive, zeroing in on universal healthcare and Medicare for All.

Bucks County Beacon — "Lucia Simonelli Wants PA-01 Democrats to Vote Their Hopes, Not Their Fears" (Apr 2026)

Simonelli makes her closing pitch as the grassroots, anti-establishment progressive in PA-01, arguing that a bold platform — Medicare for All, climate action, no corporate PAC money — gives Democrats a better shot at finally beating Fitzpatrick than a centrist approach.

WHYY — "Pa. election 2026: Meet Bob Harvie and Lucia Simonelli, Dems fighting to unseat Brian Fitzpatrick" (Apr 2026)

WHYY's primary voter guide contrasts the two candidates: Harvie's establishment endorsements and county-level executive record versus Simonelli's policy-forward, climate- and healthcare-centered platform. Notes the DCCC has declined to endorse in the primary.

Central Bucks News — "Fitzpatrick has $7M more on hand than Democrats challenging him" (Apr 23, 2026)

Simonelli raised just under $25,000 from January through March, bringing her total to roughly $50,000 since entering the race — far behind Harvie's $1.35M and Fitzpatrick's $7.65M war chest.

Pennsylvania State Senate

Steve Santarsiero — 10th Senatorial District (Incumbent)

Has represented the 10th District since 2019 and serves as chair of the Bucks County Democrats. Running unopposed in the May primary. A former teacher and lawyer, Santarsiero previously served four terms in the PA House (31st District, 2009–2016) and is active on minimum wage, PECO rate increases, and other legislative issues.

Bucks County Beacon — "State Senator Steve Santarsiero: Raising Pennsylvania's $7.25 Minimum Wage Is About Economic Justice" (Mar 2026 / ongoing)

Santarsiero continues his push to raise PA's $7.25 minimum wage — unchanged since 2009 — framing it as both economic justice and a competitiveness issue, with Pennsylvania now lagging every neighboring state.

Eileen Hartnett Albillar — 6th Senatorial District

Bucks County Clerk of Courts who launched her bid in January 2026 to unseat Republican Frank Farry. A former Warrington Township supervisor with a background in social work and nonprofit leadership, she has been endorsed by Senate Democratic Leader Jay Costa, Sen. Santarsiero, and Commissioners Harvie and Ellis-Marseglia. Her platform focuses on affordability, SEPTA funding, and working families.

Spotlight PA — "Pennsylvania legislative primaries to watch in 2026" (Apr 2026)

Spotlight PA flags the 6th District as a top Democratic flip target, with Hartnett Albillar uncontested in the primary and Senate Democrats targeting Farry's seat as one of four GOP Senate districts in their path to chamber control.

16th Senatorial District — Contested Primary

A contested Democratic primary in the 16th District (parts of Upper Bucks and Lehigh) features Lehigh County Controller Mark Pinsley and Pennridge School Board member Bradley Merkl-Gump (39, of Perkasie, an eighth-grade social studies teacher). The winner faces Republican incumbent Sen. Jarrett Coleman in November, with Democrats viewing the seat as critical for flipping the state Senate.

Armchair Lehigh Valley — "Two Democrats left in 16th Senate District's primary" (Apr 2026)

After Wayne Codner's exit, Pinsley and Merkl-Gump are now the remaining Democrats on the May 19 ballot in SD-16, setting up a contrast between an experienced county-level controller and a younger educator/school board reformer.

Spotlight PA — "Pennsylvania legislative primaries to watch in 2026" (Apr 2026)

SD-16 ranked among the most pivotal primaries in the commonwealth — one of four GOP-held seats Democrats are targeting in their bid for state Senate control, with Coleman seen as vulnerable in a redrawn, suburban-leaning seat.

Pennsylvania State House

Bryan Allen — 18th District (Bensalem)

Former Bensalem Township council member and chief of staff for State Rep. Tina Davis, age 47. Introduced the first casino tax rebate for Bensalem residents and serves as vice chair of the Bucks County Water and Sewer Authority Board. Challenging Republican incumbent K.C. Tomlinson in HD-18.

Bensalem Weekly — "Bensalem 18th District Election 2026: Allen vs Tomlinson Coverage" (Apr 2026)

Local Bensalem coverage frames the Allen-Tomlinson matchup around the township school district's projected $17M budget deficit, with Allen pressing public-education funding and property tax relief through increased state support.

Jim Prokopiak — 140th District (Falls Township), Incumbent

Won the seat in a February 2024 special election. A former Falls Township supervisor (2002–2009) and Temple University law graduate, Prokopiak is the sole Democrat on the primary ballot in HD-140 (Falls Township, Morrisville, Tullytown, parts of Middletown).

Tina Davis — 141st District (Bristol Township), Incumbent

Has represented Bristol Township and Bristol Borough since 2010. Priorities include economic development, pay equity, and property tax reform. Faces Republican Kenneth James Velez in November.

Kristin Egan — 142nd District (Langhorne)

Neshaminy School Board director and 20-year public school teacher challenging Republican Joe Hogan. The first state candidate to publicly oppose the $227M PennDOT US-1 reconstruction project. Platform: education funding reform, public safety, and cost of living.

Bucks County Beacon — "Democratic State House Candidate Kristin Egan Goes on Record to Oppose Controversial US 1 Project in Lower Bucks County" (Apr 2026)

Egan formalizes her opposition to PennDOT's US-1 reconstruction (RC3), a multi-hundred-million-dollar project unpopular with many lower Bucks residents — sharpening her contrast with Hogan, who has not committed to oppose it.

Tim Hayes — 143rd District (Dublin)

Dublin Borough Council president, Army National Guard veteran, and environmental policy expert running against Republican incumbent Shelby Labs. Priorities include alternative energy, regulating AI data farms, affordability, and education.

Bucks County Beacon — "OPINION: State House Bill Seeks to Protect Pennsylvania Communities from Data Center Impacts" (Apr 2026)

Hayes leans into the data-center issue after Republican incumbent Shelby Labs voted against a bipartisan PA House bill to set guardrails on data center development — tying it to soaring utility bills and a 38% jump in 2025 utility shutoffs across Pennsylvania.

Brian Munroe — 144th District (Warminster), Incumbent

Former Radnor Township police officer and Warminster Township supervisor. Unseated Republican Todd Polinchock in 2022 by 515 votes and was re-elected in 2024 by 1,065 votes. Endorsed by Conservation Voters of PA. HD-144 (Warminster, Warrington, Ivyland, parts of New Britain) remains a key swing district.

No new coverage in the last 48 hours. (Munroe will face Republican Michael J. Murphy of Warrington in November.)

Les Mavus — 145th District (Nockamixon)

Nockamixon Township resident and inspector of elections challenging Republican incumbent Craig Staats in HD-145 (Upper Bucks, including Quakertown, Riegelsville, and Trumbauersville).

No new coverage in the last 48 hours.

Monica Weninger — 178th District (Warwick)

25-year public school science teacher and Science Department Chair at Wissahickon School District. Auditor in Warwick Township — the first Democrat elected to that office in decades. Challenging Republican incumbent Kristin Marcell.

No new coverage in the last 48 hours.

Key Races to Watch

Spotlight PA — "Control of Pennsylvania legislature on the line in 2026" (Apr 2026)

Republicans currently hold a 27-23 edge in the state Senate. Democrats are targeting four even-year GOP seats — Farry (SD-6, Bucks), Pennycuick (SD-24, Bucks/Mont), Coleman (SD-16, Lehigh/Bucks), and Brown (SD-40, Monroe) — in a bid for chamber control. A 25-25 tie hands procedural control to Lt. Gov. Austin Davis, but a true majority requires +3.

WHYY — "PA election 2026: Primary candidates and voter info" (Apr 2026)

Pennsylvania's closed primary is Tuesday, May 19. Last day to register: Monday, May 4. Mail/absentee ballot applications due 5 p.m. Tuesday, May 12. Completed mail ballots must be received by 8 p.m. on Election Day.
