Mail-ballot cure policy ("notice and cure")
How Bucks notifiesWhen a returned mail ballot has a correctible defect, the county notifies the voter by postcard, phone and email.
How to cureCure in person at the Board of Elections, 55 E. Court St., Doylestown — or vote by provisional ballot at your polling place on Election Day.
Standard correctible defectsMissing signature · missing date · "naked ballot" (no secrecy envelope) · improperly sealed/opened envelope.
Cure deadline + litigation contextCure deadline is 8 PM on Election Day — Bucks has no post-Election-Day cure window. Statewide litigation over undated-ballot rejection continues; per AP (Aug 24 story below), the U.S. Supreme Court separately ruled in June that states may count ballots arriving after Election Day — but Pennsylvania law still requires receipt by 8 PM on Election Day, so nothing changes for Bucks voters yet. County challenge-procedure page: Mail-in/Absentee Ballot Challenges.
