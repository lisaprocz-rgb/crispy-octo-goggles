This Week at a Glance
This week at a glance:
  • Harvie won PA-1. Commissioner Bob Harvie (D, Vice Chair) defeated five-term Republican Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick on Nov 3, flipping Pennsylvania's 1st Congressional District; the AP called it Nov 4. He is now Congressman-elect and still holds his commissioner seat for now.
  • A commissioner vacancy is coming. Because PA fills a commissioner vacancy by judicial appointment (not a special election), the Bucks County Court of Common Pleas judges will pick Harvie's Democratic replacement. As of Nov 6, court administrator Stephen Watson said no application process or vote date had been set.
  • Fitzpatrick's bid to remove Marseglia from the Election Board failed. On Nov 2, visiting Chester County Judge Jeffrey R. Sommer denied Fitzpatrick's petition to remove Commissioner Diane Ellis-Marseglia (D) from the Board of Elections, writing "Without statutory authority we decline."
  • Board is Dem 2–1 until the vacancy is filled. Marseglia (D, Chair) and Gene DiGirolamo (R, Secretary) continue; the judges' appointee must be a registered Democrat to match Harvie's seat.
  • All 9 row offices remain Democratic following the January 2026 inauguration — the first time Democrats have held every Bucks row office.
  • Watch items steady: Treasurer Mark Moffa still serving in an acting capacity; Register of Wills still led by Acting First Deputy Douglas Wayne; the state's internet-connected pollbook pilot remains shelved; the dismissed county climate suit remains on appeal.
  • Holiday week. No Commissioners' or Board of Elections meeting is on the calendar for Thanksgiving week; the next regular Commissioners' meeting falls on Wednesday, Dec 2.

Mail-ballot "notice and cure" policy

How Bucks notifies: Voters whose returned mail ballots have a correctible defect are notified by postcard, phone, and email.

How to cure: In person at the Board of Elections, 55 E. Court St., Doylestown — or by voting a provisional ballot at the polls on Election Day.

Standard correctible defects: missing signature, missing date, naked ballot (no secrecy envelope), or an improperly opened/sealed outer envelope.

Cure deadline & litigation context: Cure must be completed by 8 PM on Election Day — Bucks offers no post-Election-Day cure window. The dating of mail-ballot envelopes remains legally contested statewide; it figured in the backdrop to the Oct–Nov 2026 Fitzpatrick/Marseglia dispute, though that petition itself turned on board-member bias, not ballot dating.
