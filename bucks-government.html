Mail-Ballot CURE Policy

How Bucks notifies voters

Bucks operates a "notice and cure" system. When a returned mail ballot has a correctible defect, the Board of Elections office notifies the voter by postcard, phone call, and email (when an email is on file). The notice describes the defect and explains the two cure options.

How to cure

The voter may either (a) cure in person at the Board of Elections office at 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown, or (b) vote a provisional ballot at their assigned polling place on Election Day, which is then counted in lieu of the defective mail ballot after canvass review.

Standard correctible defects

  • Missing voter signature on the outer return envelope
  • Missing date next to the signature
  • "Naked" ballot — ballot returned without its secrecy envelope
  • Improperly opened or torn outer return envelope

Cure deadline + litigation context

The cure window in Bucks closes at 8 p.m. Election Day — there is no post-Election Day cure period in Bucks. This is the policy that drew the November 2024 commissioners' controversy and the 2025 Commonwealth Court litigation over undated mail ballots; Marseglia and Harvie's position has not changed heading into the May 19 primary.
