Ballot Cure & Defect Correction Policy

How Bucks County Notifies Voters of Defects:

  • Postcard notification to the voter's address on file
  • Phone call notification when a phone number is available
  • Email notification when an email address is on file

How to Cure a Defective Mail Ballot:

  • In Person (Preferred): Visit the Bucks County Board of Elections, 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown PA 18901, during business hours (M–F 8 AM–5 PM). Voter corrects the ballot in person with election staff present.
  • At Polls on Election Day: Cast a provisional ballot at your assigned polling location if you cannot cure before Election Day.
  • NO Mail/Phone Cure: Bucks County does not permit cure by mail or phone. In-person correction or provisional voting are the only remedies.

Standard Correctible Defects (Notice & Cure Eligible):

  • Missing voter signature on the outside envelope
  • Missing or illegible date on the outside envelope (undated or incorrectly dated ballot)
  • Improperly opened envelope (e.g., torn, contents visible)
  • Naked ballot (ballot submitted without the official security envelope)

Cure Deadline & Litigation Context:

  • Cure deadline is 8 PM on Election Day (no post-Election Day cure window in Bucks County)
  • Voters must be notified and given opportunity to cure BEFORE Election Day
  • Provision for provisional voting ensures all voters can participate even if cure is not possible

🔍 PA Internet-Connected Pollbook Pilot Program — POSTPONED

Status as of June 2026: The Pennsylvania Department of State announced in February 2026 that it would pilot internet-connected electronic pollbooks in counties during the May 2026 primary election. However, in May 2026, the pilot was postponed to at least 2027 due to unresolved technical questions and low county participation.

Bucks County Participation: Bucks County did NOT sign up for the pilot. Only Philadelphia, Lebanon, and Delaware counties expressed interest. Bucks continues to use KnowInk Poll Pad devices on a closed, non-internet-connected network.

🏆 The Democratic Sweep — January 6, 2026 Investiture

Four new judges were sworn in on January 6, 2026, in a historic investiture ceremony representing the first complete Democratic sweep of Bucks County's judicial election. All four bring significant experience across family law, criminal prosecution, housing, land use, and public service.

Judge Linda Bobrin — Former Register of Wills and Clerk of Orphans' Court. Bobrin resigned her elected position to assume her seat as a judge.

Bucks County Beacon — Bucks County's 'Blue Wave' Makes a Splash With Swearing In of New Row Officers (January 2026)
The historic swearing-in of five new row officers and four judges marked Bucks County's transition to an all-Democratic row office slate.

Judge Tiffany M. Thomas-Smith — The first Black woman ever to serve on the Bucks County Court of Common Pleas. Thomas-Smith brings extensive experience in family law and community advocacy.

Judge Dawn DiDonato Burke — Sworn in on the same date as the other three. Burke brings experience in criminal law and courtroom advocacy.

Judge Amy M. Fitzpatrick — Former Bucks County Solicitor (served as Assistant County Solicitor 2020–2023 and as County Solicitor thereafter). IMPORTANT: Judge Fitzpatrick is NOT related to U.S. Representative Brian Fitzpatrick (R-PA-1). She represented healthcare clients at Buchanan Ingersoll & Rooney PC before her government service.
