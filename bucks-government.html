This Week at a Glance
  • Preliminary 2027 budget drops with no tax increase (Nov 25). The commissioners released a $521.6M operating plan that holds the millage rate steady at 29.65 mills (avg. bill ~$960/household) — a 0.7% spending bump over 2026. CFO Jeannette Weaver led the build; the board votes on a final budget in December.
  • A commissioners' vacancy is now looming. Vice-Chair Bob Harvie (D) unseated U.S. Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick in PA-1 on Nov 3 and will resign the county seat before being sworn into Congress in January 2027. Under state law, the Court of Common Pleas judges — not the voters — will appoint his replacement.
  • Harvie still holds the commissioner seat and chairs the Board of Elections as of this run; no resignation date has been published.
  • Standing litigation steady: a judge's early-November rejection of Fitzpatrick's bid to remove Diane Ellis-Marseglia (D) from the Board of Elections stands; the county's climate appeal remains pending before Commonwealth Court.
  • Row offices: all nine remain Democratic following the January 2026 swearing-in; Treasurer Mark Moffa continues in an acting capacity and the Register of Wills remains under acting First Deputy Douglas Wayne.

Mail-ballot cure policy
How Bucks notifies voters Bucks operates a "notice and cure" process: voters with a correctable defect are notified by postcard, phone, and email.
How to cure Cure in person at 55 E. Court St., Doylestown, OR vote a provisional ballot at the polls.
Standard correctable defects Missing signature, missing date, naked ballot (no inner secrecy envelope), or an improperly opened outer envelope.
Cure deadline & litigation context The cure deadline is 8 PM on Election Day — Bucks provides no post-Election-Day cure window. Undated/misdated-ballot policy remains the subject of ongoing statewide litigation; see standing watch items.
