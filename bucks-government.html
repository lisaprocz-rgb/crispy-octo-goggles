Mail-ballot cure policy

How Bucks notifies voters

Bucks County operates a "notice and cure" process. When a returned mail ballot has a correctible defect, the Board of Elections notifies the voter by postcard, phone, and/or email.

How to cure

A voter may cure in person at the Board of Elections, 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown, or vote by provisional ballot at their polling place on Election Day.

Standard correctible defects

Missing signature, missing date on the voter declaration, a "naked ballot" (no inner secrecy envelope), and an improperly opened or sealed return envelope.

Cure deadline & litigation context

The cure deadline is 8 p.m. on Election Day — Bucks does not offer a post-Election-Day cure window. The county's posture on undated and incorrectly dated ballots has drawn litigation and a prior court order; the appeal landscape on undated-ballot counting remains a live statewide issue.

PA internet-connected pollbook pilot — watch item

The Pennsylvania Department of State has postponed its pilot program to connect electronic pollbooks to the internet; counties will not test the function in the May 19 primary. Officials cited unresolved technical questions and low county interest — only Philadelphia, Lebanon and Delaware counties had signed up. Bucks County was not among the participating counties, which definitively answers this season's open question about Bucks's participation. The Department says it will pursue a more comprehensive feasibility review of full connectivity after the 2026 General Election (Votebeat, May 12; Spotlight PA / Pennsylvania Capital-Star / LevittownNow, May 12–13).
