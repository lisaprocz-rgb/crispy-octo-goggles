Mail-Ballot Cure Policy

Overview: Bucks County operates a "notice and cure" process. Voters whose mail ballots contain errors are notified by the Board and given an opportunity to fix ("cure") the ballot before Election Day. Cure requests must be made by 8 PM on Election Day — there is no post-Election Day cure window in Bucks County.

How Bucks Notifies Voters: The Board contacts voters by postcard, phone call, and email to alert them of ballot defects.

How to Cure Your Ballot (Four Options):

  1. In-Person at Board Office (55 E. Court St, Doylestown): Election officials will allow you to sign, date, or otherwise fix the ballot on the spot.
  2. Cancel & Replace in One Visit: Visit the Board, complete a cancel-and-replace form, receive a new blank ballot, and return it completed the same day.
  3. Provisional Ballot at Polls: If notified of an error but unable to visit the Board, vote a provisional ballot at your polling place on Election Day.
  4. Not Available: Bucks County does not offer a post-Election Day cure window. Cure requests must be resolved by 8 PM Election Day.

Standard Correctible Defects (Curable): Missing signature, missing or incorrect date, "naked ballot" (ballot without secrecy envelope), improperly opened or sealed envelope.

Undated Ballots Litigation Context: Following Pennsylvania Supreme Court rulings, the parties in ongoing Bucks County election litigation agreed (as of November 2024) that the Board shall not count any undated or wrongly dated mail-in ballots. Bucks County continues to emphasize the importance of proper dating and signature on all mail ballots. Recent studies show that ballot curing significantly increases mail-ballot counts across Pennsylvania.
