This Week at a Glance
This week at a glance:
  • DA's office had the busiest week of any county office. DA Joe Khan announced a guilty verdict in the armed robbery of a Doylestown produce market (Nov 20), a guilty verdict in a child sexual assault case (Nov 19), a Kentucky man's guilty plea in a Bucks County sex-trafficking case (Nov 19), and two new detectives plus an intelligence analyst joining the Detective Division (Nov 18).
  • Harvie's commissioner seat. Commissioner Chair Bob Harvie (D) won U.S. House seat PA-1, defeating GOP Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick on Nov 3. Once he leaves to be sworn into Congress (Jan 3, 2027), the Court of Common Pleas judges — led by President Judge Raymond McHugh — will appoint a Democrat to serve the balance of his term (to January 2028). As of the latest coverage, no application deadline, vote date, or successor had been set.
  • 2026 budget (context, Nov 9). The Board of Commissioners enacted a $517 million 2026 operating budget, raising real estate taxes by 2.2 mills (~$6/month for the average household) to close a $16.4M deficit after the state budget impasse and the federal shutdown.
  • Election Board litigation resolved. A Common Pleas judge rejected Brian Fitzpatrick's pre-election bid to remove Vice Chair Diane Ellis-Marseglia (D) from the Bucks County Board of Elections.
  • General election closed out. The Board of Elections finished the Nov 3 general election, in which Democrats won the governorship and a legislative trifecta statewide and the AP called PA-1 for Harvie.
  • Standing watch. Pennsylvania's internet-connected e-pollbook pilot remains postponed (it did not run in 2026); the county's dismissed climate lawsuit is still on appeal in Commonwealth Court; and Treasurer Mark Moffa and the Register of Wills office both remain in acting status.

Mail-ballot cure policy
How Bucks notifies voters Bucks operates a "notice and cure" process: voters with a correctible defect are notified by postcard, phone, and/or email.
How to cure A voter can cure in person at the Board of Elections, 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown — or vote a provisional ballot at their polling place on Election Day.
Standard correctible defects Missing signature, missing date, a "naked" ballot (no secrecy envelope), or an improperly opened/sealed envelope.
Cure deadline & litigation context The cure deadline in Bucks is 8 PM on Election Day — there is no post-Election-Day cure window. On the underlying date-requirement fight, a federal appeals court held Pennsylvania's handwritten-date mandate unconstitutional, the U.S. Supreme Court declined to disturb mail-voting procedures for 2026, and Gov. Shapiro said mail voting would be unchanged for 2026 (Pennsylvania Capital-Star).
