🗞️ This week at a glance
  • Commissioner Bob Harvie (D) won Congress. The AP and ABC News projected Harvie the winner over GOP Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick in PA-01 overnight Nov 3–4 — ~49.1% to 44.3% (180,036 to 162,454, a ~17,500-vote margin at 91% reporting). It flips a seat Republicans held for over a decade and was the last of PA's four battleground U.S. House races to be called.
  • Harvie's win sets up a Bucks commissioner vacancy. He remains a sitting commissioner (and Board of Elections chair) until he resigns to be sworn into Congress in January 2027. Under the PA County Code, the Court of Common Pleas judges will then appoint a Democrat to preserve the board's 2–1 majority — a county-government storyline to watch.
  • Clerk of Courts Eileen Hartnett Albillar (D) is leading for the PA Senate. In early returns in the 6th Senatorial District she led GOP incumbent Frank Farry; the race had not been officially called as of this run. A win would vacate her county row office.
  • Judge rejected Fitzpatrick's bid to remove Commissioner Diane Ellis-Marseglia from the Board of Elections (Nov 2). Visiting Chester County Judge Jeffrey R. Sommer: "Without statutory authority we decline." The fight is effectively moot now that voting has ended.
  • Mail ballots came in heavy. Bucks reported 94,892 absentee/mail ballots received by the 8 PM Election Day deadline (unofficial). The Board of Elections began its pre-canvass at 7 AM Nov 3 and moves to the canvass/computation of returns this week.
  • Statewide context: Gov. Josh Shapiro won re-election and Democrats took control of the U.S. House; the Pennsylvania legislature's control hinged on a handful of seats including Bucks's 6th Senate district.
  • Standing Watch: the climate-suit appeal, Sheriff Ceisler's ICE withdrawal, Treasurer Moffa's pending confirmation, and the Register of Wills vacancy show no new developments this week.

Mail-ballot cure policy
How Bucks handles defective mail ballots Bucks operates a "notice and cure" process for correctible mail-ballot defects.
  • How the county notifies voters: by postcard, phone, and email when a returned ballot has a correctible defect.
  • How to cure: in person at 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown, or by voting a provisional ballot at the polls.
  • Standard correctible defects: missing signature, missing date, naked ballot (no secrecy envelope), or an improperly opened/sealed envelope.
  • Cure deadline: 8 PM on Election Day — now passed for the 2026 General. Bucks has no post-Election-Day cure window; provisional ballots cast at the polls are now part of the canvass.

Litigation context: the handling of undated/misdated and provisional ballots has been repeatedly litigated in Bucks and statewide, and Commissioner Ellis-Marseglia's 2024 remarks on a disputed ballot vote were central to the pre-election removal challenge described above.
