Mail-ballot CURE policy — what voters can do if their ballot has an error

Bucks County is one of the Pennsylvania counties that operates a "notice and cure" process for mail ballots with defects. After a contested decade in PA courts, the September 2025 Pennsylvania Supreme Court ruling (4-3) now requires all 67 counties to notify voters of rejected ballots — Bucks already did this and goes further than the bare minimum.

How Bucks notifies voters of defects

  • Postcard mailed to the voter's registered address.
  • Direct phone call from the BOE to the voter.
  • Email through the PA Department of State ballot tracking system.

How a voter can cure (fix) a defective ballot

  • In person: Visit the Board of Elections office at 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown. Tell the official the ballot was rejected; staff will pull the ballot and let the voter add a missing signature, missing/incorrect date, or other correctible defect.
  • At the polls on Election Day: Voters notified that their mail ballot has an uncurable defect can cast a PROVISIONAL ballot at their normal polling place; provisional ballots are reviewed during the post-election canvass.
  • Phone the BOE at 215-348-6154 to confirm the specific defect before traveling.

Standard correctible defects

  • Missing voter signature on the outer return envelope.
  • Missing handwritten DATE on the outer return envelope (the focus of years of PA litigation).
  • Naked ballot — outer envelope sealed but inner secrecy envelope was forgotten. Can be returned and re-issued in some cases.
  • Improperly opened envelope (rare, but recoverable).

Cure deadline (per BOE practice)

  • Cure must be completed by 8 p.m. on Election Day (May 19) — the same hard deadline as ballot return. There is no post-Election Day cure window in Bucks. Voters notified late should consider voting provisionally at the polls instead of trying to cure.

Litigation context the reader should know

  • September 2025 — PA Supreme Court (4-3) ruled that ALL counties must notify voters of mail-ballot defects. Bucks already did, but the ruling locks in the practice statewide.
  • November 2024 — PA Supreme Court (4-3) reaffirmed that counties cannot count incorrectly dated or undated ballots and EXPLICITLY rebuked the boards of elections in Bucks, Montgomery and Philadelphia. Bucks's BOE — chaired by Bob Harvie, with Marseglia voting to count — had voted 2-1 to tally 405 undated ballots in the McCormick-Casey U.S. Senate race. DiGirolamo voted no. The episode is still actively cited by GOP critics.
  • Practical effect: Bucks's BOE will count properly dated and signed ballots only. Cure is the safety valve for voters whose envelopes are defective.
