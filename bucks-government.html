📌 This week at a glance:
  • Deputy pay-raise dispute (Jun 18): At the June 18 Commissioners' business meeting, the board addressed how pay increases and job-classification upgrades for county deputies were processed before commissioners signed the union contract. Under state law, commissioners are the only officials who may sign such contracts; they have since authorized the raises.
  • DA Khan's tech lawsuit expands: Bucks County became the first DA's office in the nation to sue X Corp. (formerly Twitter), adding Roblox, Discord and Meta Platforms Technologies to its federal child-safety suit targeting "experimental" AI chatbots — including X's Grok.
  • Grok CSAM arrest: Harry Tiffany IV, 66, of New Britain Borough was charged June 9 with felony child-sexual-abuse-material counts after NCMEC CyberTips flagged 37 Grok-generated image files (search warrant executed June 5).
  • Pollbook pilot dead for 2026: PA's Dept. of State postponed its internet-connected e-pollbook pilot to at least 2027. Bucks never signed up — only Philadelphia, Lebanon and Delaware counties did.
  • 2026 primary certified: The May 19 primary is complete; Bucks has published certified summary and precinct returns. Roughly 51,452 mail/absentee ballots were received by the 8 PM deadline.
  • Climate appeal pending: Bucks's appeal of Judge Corr's dismissal sits before Commonwealth Court; AG Dave Sunday, the U.S. Chamber and a civil-justice coalition filed April amicus briefs backing dismissal.

Mail-ballot CURE policy

How Bucks notifies: "Notice and cure" — voters with a correctible defect are contacted by postcard, phone, and/or email.

How to cure: In person at 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown, OR by voting a provisional ballot at the polls.

Standard correctible defects: missing signature, missing date, naked ballot (no secrecy envelope), or improperly opened envelope.

Cure deadline: 8 PM on Election Day — Bucks has no post-Election-Day cure window. Undated/misdated mail ballots remain the subject of ongoing statewide litigation.
