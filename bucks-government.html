Mail-Ballot Cure Policy
How Bucks notifies voters of defects:

BOE staff send a postcard, then call and/or email when reachable. Notifications are also visible in the PA mail-ballot tracker at pavoterservices.pa.gov.

How a voter cures:

Two routes — (1) come in person to 55 E. Court St., Doylestown, before 8 PM Election Day to sign / date / replace the envelope, or (2) vote a provisional ballot at the polling place. Bucks does not offer a post-Election Day cure window.

Standard correctible defects:
  • Missing voter signature on the declaration envelope
  • Missing or incorrect date
  • "Naked" ballot (no inner secrecy envelope)
  • Improperly opened / damaged outer envelope
Cure deadline & litigation context:

8 PM Tuesday, May 19 is the hard cutoff. The Pennsylvania Supreme Court's late-2024 ruling — that undated and misdated mail ballots cannot be counted — remains controlling law. Republican plaintiffs forced Bucks into a stipulation on Nov 19, 2024 not to count such ballots; that stipulation continues to bind the BOE for the May 19 primary. No new state ruling has displaced it this cycle. Read more at Democracy Docket →

Watch: PA internet-connected pollbook pilot
Pilot postponed — Bucks unaffected this cycle The Pennsylvania Department of State announced on May 12, 2026 that it is postponing the internet-connected e-pollbook pilot scheduled for the May 19 primary, citing unresolved technical questions and low county interest. Only Philadelphia, Lebanon, and Delaware counties had signed up; Bucks was not among them. DoS now plans a feasibility review after the November general, with the earliest possible pilot in 2027.
