  • Harvie headed to Congress; a commissioner vacancy is coming. Vice-Chair Bob Harvie (D) defeated Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick (R) in Pennsylvania's 1st Congressional District (election Nov 3). Under state law the Court of Common Pleas judges, led by President Judge Raymond McHugh, will appoint a Democrat to finish Harvie's term (to Jan 2028); as of early November the court had not finalized the application window or vote.

Frank Farry (R) in the 6th Senate District (Farry conceded), part of Democrats flipping the Pennsylvania Senate — foreshadowing a looming Clerk of Courts vacancy once she is seated in Harrisburg.

  • Election-board challenge failed. A Bucks judge rejected Fitzpatrick's pre-election bid to remove Commissioner Diane Ellis-Marseglia from the Board of Elections (ruling Nov 2–3).
  • Public health: the Bucks County Health Department reported a measles case in the county (Nov 9).
  • Budget season opens. The Board of Commissioners meets next on Dec 2 and Dec 16; the county has historically proposed its operating budget in late November and adopted it in December/January.
  • Quiet on the standing watches: no movement this week on the climate-suit appeal, Treasurer Moffa's Senate confirmation, or the permanent Register of Wills fill.

Mail-ballot cure policy
How Bucks notifies votersBucks operates a "notice and cure" process: voters with a correctable defect are notified by postcard, phone, and/or email.
How to cureCure in person at 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown, or vote a provisional ballot at the polls on Election Day.
Standard correctable defectsMissing signature, missing date, naked ballot (no inner secrecy envelope), or an improperly opened/sealed outer envelope.
Cure deadline8 PM on Election Day — Bucks does not offer a post-Election-Day cure window. Undated/misdated-ballot handling remains the subject of ongoing statewide litigation.
