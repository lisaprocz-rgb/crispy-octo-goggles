This Week at a Glance
The big threads right now:
  • Two pending appointments dominate the county. The Nov. 3 general election produced two vacancies that the Court of Common Pleas must fill by appointment — Commissioner Bob Harvie's seat and Clerk of Courts Eileen Hartnett Albillar's seat. Both must be filled by registered Democrats.
  • Harvie (D) defeated U.S. Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick in PA-1 (called Nov. 4), flipping a seat Republicans held for over a decade. He leaves the Board of Commissioners when the new Congress is sworn in Jan. 3, 2027.
  • Clerk of Courts Albillar (D) narrowly beat Sen. Frank Farry (R) in the 6th Senate District; Farry conceded. Her win helped Democrats capture a PA Senate majority and a Harrisburg "trifecta." Her expected move to the Senate opens the second row-office vacancy.
  • A Bucks judge rejected Fitzpatrick's pre-election bid to remove Commissioner Diane Ellis-Marseglia from the Board of Elections (Nov. 2), leaving the three-commissioner board intact through the count.
  • As of Nov. 6, the Court of Common Pleas had set no application process, deadline, or vote date for Harvie's replacement; President Judge Raymond McHugh would break any tie among the judges.
  • Budget season: commissioners move toward adopting the 2027 county budget in December (the 2026 budget carried an ~8% property-tax increase).
  • Standing watch: the climate suit Bucks County v. BP remains on appeal in Commonwealth Court; Pennsylvania's internet-connected pollbook pilot stayed postponed statewide; Treasurer Mark Moffa is still serving in an acting capacity.

Mail-ballot cure policy
Bucks operates "notice and cure" How Bucks notifies: voters with a defective mail ballot are contacted by postcard, phone, and/or email. How to cure: in person at 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown, OR by voting a provisional ballot at the polls on Election Day. Standard correctible defects: missing signature, missing date, "naked" ballot (no secrecy envelope), or an improperly sealed/opened outer envelope. Cure deadline: 8:00 PM on Election Day — Bucks offers no post-Election-Day cure window. The undated-ballot question remains the subject of ongoing statewide litigation.
