Mail-Ballot Cure Policy

How Bucks notifies voters

Bucks County operates a "notice and cure" process: when a returned mail ballot has a correctible defect, the Board of Elections notifies the voter by postcard, phone, and/or email.

How to cure

A voter can cure in person at the Board of Elections, 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown — staff pull the ballot and let the voter fix the error (e.g., add a missing signature or date) — or instead vote a provisional ballot at their polling place on Election Day.

Standard correctible defects

Missing voter signature, missing date on the outer return envelope, a "naked" ballot (no inner secrecy envelope), and an improperly opened or sealed envelope.

Cure deadline & litigation context

The cure deadline in Bucks is 8 PM on Election Day — there is no post-Election Day cure window. The dating requirement for mail-ballot envelopes has flipped on and off through litigation in recent years and remains an active statewide issue heading into the May 19 primary.

PA Internet-Connected Pollbook Pilot — Watch Item

The Pennsylvania Department of State has postponed its planned pilot to connect electronic pollbooks to the internet — originally slated for the May 19 primary — until at least 2027, citing unresolved technical questions and low county interest. Only Philadelphia, Lebanon and Delaware counties had signed up. Bucks County was never a participant, so the postponement does not change anything operationally in Bucks for this primary (Votebeat / Spotlight PA, May 12, 2026).
