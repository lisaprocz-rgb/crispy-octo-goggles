Mail-ballot cure policy

How Bucks notifies voters

Bucks operates an active "notice and cure" program: when a returned mail ballot is flagged for a correctible defect, the BOE notifies the voter by postcard, phone call, and/or email using the contact info on the ballot application.

How to cure

Voters may either (a) appear in person at the BOE office, 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown, to correct the defect, or (b) vote a provisional ballot at their polling place on Election Day.

Standard correctible defects

Missing signature on the voter declaration; missing date; "naked" ballot (no inner secrecy envelope); improperly opened or sealed return envelope.

Cure deadline + litigation context

Bucks's cure deadline is 8:00 PM on Election Day (May 19). There is no post-Election-Day cure window in Bucks. Commissioners Harvie and Marseglia previously voted to count undated/incorrectly dated ballots in 2024 in defiance of court orders — the PA Supreme Court ultimately ruled 7-0 against that approach. Their posture for 2026 is to follow the current state-law guidance on dating and signature requirements while continuing to provide active notice and cure for correctible defects.

⚖️ Court of Common Pleas

Bucks's bench saw a historic Democratic sweep in November 2025 — four new judges (all Democrats) defeated the GOP ticket of Deon/Jackman/Pizzo/Monahan and were sworn in January 6, 2026 in an investiture at the Justice Center. President Judge Raymond F. McHugh continues to administer the court.

Four new judges (sworn 1/6/2026)

  • Linda Bobrin — formerly elected Register of Wills (resigned to take judgeship); first Jewish woman elected to the Bucks bench.
  • Dawn DiDonato Burke — three decades as an attorney; former Philadelphia District Attorney's Office prosecutor.
  • Tiffany M. Thomas-Smith — first Black woman ever to serve on the Bucks bench; began her career as a Philadelphia public defender.
  • Amy M. Fitzpatrick — former Bucks County Solicitor and national-law-firm attorney. NOT related to U.S. Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick (R-PA-1) — common-surname coincidence.

Judges in the news this week

Judge Stephen A. Corr

PA Attorney General David Sunday filed an amicus brief in Commonwealth Court on May 1 supporting Judge Corr's dismissal of Bucks County's climate-tort lawsuit against BP, Chevron, ConocoPhillips, ExxonMobil, Phillips 66, Shell and the American Petroleum Institute. Sunday argues federal law preempts the suit. The U.S. Chamber of Commerce and PA Coalition for Civil Justice Reform filed parallel amicus briefs the same week. The county's appeal remains pending.
