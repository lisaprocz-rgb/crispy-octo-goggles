🟢 This week at a glance
  • Bob Harvie (D) won Pennsylvania's 1st Congressional District, ousting five-term Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick (R) in the Nov. 3 general election — the AP called it Nov. 4 with Harvie leading 49.1%–44.3% (~17,582-vote margin, 91% counted; figures unofficial pending certification). Harvie keeps his Bucks commissioner seat until he is sworn into Congress in January 2027.
  • A coming vacancy looms on the 3-member Board of Commissioners — and in Harvie's separate role as Board of Elections chair — once he resigns to join Congress. No successor or fill process has been announced this week.
  • The Board of Elections is canvassing the Nov. 3 general (provisional and mail-ballot adjudication); county certification is due under Pennsylvania's ~20-day deadline, on or about Nov. 24.
  • Pennsylvania's internet-connected e-pollbook pilot stays shelved — the Department of State pulled it before the 2026 cycle; Bucks was never confirmed as a participant. No change this week.
  • Sheriff Danny Ceisler (D) continues the election-non-interference posture he pledged in October; a court earlier upheld predecessor Harran's ICE agreement, which Ceisler has since ended.
  • Treasurer Mark Moffa (D) remains acting pending state Senate confirmation, and the Register of Wills office is still led by acting First Deputy Douglas Wayne (D) after Linda Bobrin left for the Common Pleas bench.
  • Statewide: the Pennsylvania Supreme Court's order barring counties from counting undated/misdated mail-ballot envelopes remains the controlling guidance Bucks follows.

Mail-ballot cure policy
How Bucks notifies Bucks operates a "notice and cure" process — voters with a correctible defect are notified by postcard, phone, and/or email.
How to cure Cure in person at 55 E. Court St., Doylestown, OR vote a provisional ballot at the polls.
Standard correctible defects Missing signature, missing date, naked ballot (no secrecy envelope), and improperly opened/sealed outer envelope.
Cure deadline & litigation context The deadline is 8 PM on Election Day — Bucks has no post-Election-Day cure window. Statewide, the Pennsylvania Supreme Court has ordered counties not to count undated or misdated mail-ballot envelopes, the guidance Bucks follows.
