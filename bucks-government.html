📨 Mail-ballot CURE policy

How Bucks notifies voters of a defective ballot

Bucks County operates a "notice and cure" policy. If a returned mail ballot has a curable defect, BOE staff will: (1) mail the voter a postcard explaining the problem, and (2) attempt to reach the voter by phone and/or email "as bandwidth permits."

How a voter can cure

Voters may cure their ballot in person at the Board of Elections at 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown. They may also surrender the defective mail ballot at their precinct on Election Day and cast a PROVISIONAL ballot at the polls — a backstop option for all PA voters.

Standard correctible defects

The four defects routinely cured under the notice-and-cure protocol are: missing signature on the outer declaration, missing date on the outer declaration, "naked" ballot (no inner secrecy envelope), and an improperly opened envelope.

Cure deadline & litigation context

Bucks has NO post-Election Day cure window — the cure deadline is 8:00 p.m. on Election Day, same as the ballot return deadline. (Marseglia and Harvie continue to be on the record favoring counting undated ballots, a position that has put them in direct conflict with state Republican officials and is one of the storylines that became national in 2024.)
