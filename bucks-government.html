Mail-ballot cure policy

Bucks operates "notice and cure" — staff contact voters whose mail ballots have correctible defects up to 5 days before Election Day, then voters can either cure in person or vote provisionally at the polls. There is no post-Election-Day cure window in Bucks.

How Bucks notifies voters Until 5 days before Election Day, BOE staff phone, email, and/or send postcards to voters whose mail ballots have a flaw that would invalidate them. The original return envelope is canceled in the state SURE system; a new correspondence code is generated and a replacement ballot is mailed with a letter explaining the defect.
How to cure a flagged ballot Two options: (1) bring identification to the BOE office at 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown, and complete a new ballot under staff supervision; or (2) vote a provisional ballot at your assigned polling place on Election Day. The provisional path is the practical fallback for voters who can't reach Doylestown in time.
Standard correctible defects Missing signature on the voter-declaration envelope · missing or incorrect date · "naked" ballot (ballot returned without the inner secrecy envelope) · improperly opened or torn return envelope · signature/identifier mismatch with the SURE record.
Cure deadline & litigation context Cure must be completed by 8 PM on Election Day. Bucks is one of the PA counties that ended on-the-record notice-and-cure programs during the 2024 cycle litigation; the 2026 program is again operating in line with prior PA Supreme Court and Commonwealth Court rulings on dated/undated envelopes.
