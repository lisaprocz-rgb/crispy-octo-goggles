This Week at a Glance
This week at a glance:
  • Bob Harvie (D) — Commissioners’ Vice Chair — defeated five-term Republican Brian Fitzpatrick in Pennsylvania’s 1st Congressional District on Nov. 3, flipping a seat the GOP had held for over a decade. He is sworn into Congress Jan. 3, 2027.
  • Harvie’s win creates a vacancy on the three-member Board of Commissioners. Under PA law the seat is filled by appointment of the Court of Common Pleas (President Judge Raymond McHugh), not by special election; the appointee must be a registered Bucks Democrat and serves the balance of Harvie’s term (through Jan. 2028).
  • As of the latest reporting (Nov. 6), no candidates have been named and the court had not finalized an application window or vote date. Dem Chair Steve Santarsiero said a replacement had “not yet” been chosen.
  • Before the election, Fitzpatrick sued to remove a Democratic member from the county Election Board; a judge denied the suit, and the board ran Tuesday’s vote as constituted.
  • Statewide context: Democrats flipped control of the U.S. House and the Pennsylvania state Senate, giving the party a Harrisburg trifecta — the backdrop to Harvie’s race.
  • All 9 row offices remain Democratic; Treasurer Mark Moffa still serves in an acting capacity pending state Senate confirmation, and the Register of Wills seat remains filled on an acting basis (First Deputy Douglas Wayne) after Linda Bobrin left for a judgeship.

6 · Mail-ballot cure policy
How Bucks notifies “Notice and cure”: voters with a correctible defect are notified by postcard, phone, and/or email.
How to cure In person at the Board of Elections, 55 E. Court St., Doylestown — or vote a provisional ballot at the polls.
Standard correctible defects Missing signature, missing date, naked ballot (no secrecy envelope), or an improperly opened/sealed outer envelope.
Cure deadline & context 8 PM on Election Day — Bucks provides no post-Election-Day cure window. Undated/mis-dated-ballot treatment remains the subject of ongoing statewide litigation; the commissioners’ posture on it is tracked in Standing Watch.
