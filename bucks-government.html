Mail-ballot CURE policy

How Bucks notifies voters of a curable defect

The BOE operates a "notice and cure" practice: voters whose mail ballots have a correctible defect are notified by postcard, phone, and (when an email is on file) email from the elections office.

How to cure

Two paths: (1) cure in person at the BOE office at 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown, during business hours (8 AM – 5 PM), where staff can reissue an envelope or witness a corrected signature/date; or (2) vote a provisional ballot at the polls on Election Day if you can't make it to Doylestown.

Standard correctible defects

  • Missing or undated voter signature on the declaration
  • Missing date on the declaration
  • Naked ballot (return envelope without secrecy envelope)
  • Improperly opened / damaged secrecy envelope

Cure deadline + litigation context

The Bucks cure deadline is 8:00 PM on Election Day (Tuesday, May 19) — the same as the ballot-return deadline. Bucks does not operate a post-Election Day cure window. The county's posture on undated/misdated ballots in 2024 drew Republican litigation (the McCormick-Casey recount challenge) that ultimately resulted in PA Supreme Court guidance directing Bucks, Montgomery, and Philadelphia not to count undated/misdated mail ballots in the 2024 count. As of this run, no analogous 2026 primary lawsuit has been filed; watch for one in the closing days before May 19.
