This Week at a Glance
This week at a glance:
  • Harvie heads to Congress, commissioner seat to open. Commissioner Bob Harvie (D) unseated five-term U.S. Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick in PA-01 on Nov. 3. His move to Washington will leave a vacancy on the three-member Board of Commissioners; the Court of Common Pleas — not a special election — will appoint a Democrat to finish the term that runs to January 2028.   • Board of Elections now chaired by a judge. With Harvie recused from the board during his congressional run, Common Pleas Judge Jordan Yeager is serving as chair alongside Ellis-Marseglia (D) and DiGirolamo (R).
  • Row offices stay all-Democratic, and the historic all-Democratic Common Pleas bench (four judges sworn Jan. 6, 2026) is intact. Treasurer Mark Moffa remains acting; the Register of Wills seat remains filled on an acting basis.

Mail-ballot "notice and cure" policy

Bucks operates a notice-and-cure process for correctible mail-ballot defects.

  • How Bucks notifies: by postcard, phone and email when a defect is detected.
  • How to cure: in person at 55 E. Court Street, Doylestown, or by voting a provisional ballot at the polls.
  • Standard correctible defects: missing signature, missing date, naked ballot (no inner secrecy envelope), or an improperly opened envelope.
  • Cure deadline: 8 PM on Election Day — Bucks has no post–Election Day cure window.
